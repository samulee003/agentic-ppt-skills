# Data slides

Use this whenever a slide shows numbers: charts, tables, or hero figures.

## One chart, one point

- The action title states the insight the chart proves ("Mobile now drives 62%
  of orders"). The chart exists to make that one sentence obviously true.
- Highlight the series or bar the title talks about in the theme's accent;
  render everything else in the muted colour.
- If the chart supports two points, make two slides.

## Choosing the form

| The point is about… | Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| change over time | line (≤ 4 series), or column for few periods | pie, 3D anything |
| ranking or comparison | horizontal bar, sorted | unsorted bars, radar |
| part of a whole | stacked bar or a single 100% bar; pie only for 2–3 parts | donut with 6+ slices |
| distribution | histogram or dot plot | average alone |
| relationship | scatter with the key points labelled | dual-axis lines |
| one number that matters | hero number with its comparison ("62%, up from 41% in 2023") | a chart around a single value |
| exact values people will look up | a table, in the reading deck or backup | a dense table in the presenting deck |

## Honesty rules

- Bar and column axes start at zero. Line charts may zoom, with the axis
  clearly labelled.
- Every chart has a source line (hard-floor size or larger) naming the source,
  date, and definition, matching its `EVIDENCE-LEDGER.md` row.
- Show the comparison that gives a number meaning: prior period, target,
  benchmark, or denominator.
- Label data directly where possible; avoid legends the audience must decode.
- Never rely on colour alone to distinguish series; add labels or patterns.

## Density

- Presenting deck: at most about 7 bars, 4 lines, or 6 table rows per slide.
- Put the full table in backup or in the reading deck, and point to it.
